The UK Government has introduced its Energy Independence Bill in Parliament. It would end new licences to explore for oil and gas in the North Sea and new onshore licences in England, including for fracking, while existing fields keep producing under new Transitional Energy Certificates. Developers would have to offer communities up to a 20% stake in new renewable projects, and councils would get stronger powers to enforce energy efficiency standards in rented homes. It is a bill, not law: the second reading has no date, the gas standing-charge cut and a publicly owned Great British Grid depend on amendments still to come, and the savings figures are the government's own. Still, ending new oil and gas licences while backing community-owned clean power is a step for the climate.
