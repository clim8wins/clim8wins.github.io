US geothermal developer Fervo Energy says it now has 1,054 MW of power sold under binding contracts, worth about US$11.9 billion, up from 658 MW three months earlier, according to its third-quarter results on November 9. Most of the rise is a 396 MW agreement with Google announced on September 1, with first power expected in mid-2028. Only 36 MW is operating so far, the first Cape Station block in Utah has run commercially since September 30, and the rest of Phase I is due by January 1, 2027. Every figure here is Fervo's own: a study putting the site's heat potential at 8.4 GW is a resource estimate, not buildable capacity, the Google expansion is only an option, and costs are above the company's targets. Still, round-the-clock clean power from underground heat is a step for the climate, if it's delivered.
