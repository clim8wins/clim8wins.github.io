During July 2026's brutal heatwave, clean energy didn't just hold up — it saved the day. Wind and solar output actually rose during the highest-demand hours, helping save the US grid more than $170 million in wholesale electricity costs, per the American Clean Power Association. And that's without the 74 gigawatts of solar, wind, and battery projects still stuck waiting to connect to the grid — bringing those online faster would save even more and help prevent reliability shortfalls next time the heat spikes.
