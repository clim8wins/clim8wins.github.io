At the UN's COP17 land conference in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, UNEP and FAO awarded three new World Restoration Flagships — in Brazil, the Sahel, and Saudi Arabia — together committing to restore almost 5 million hectares by 2030, roughly 7 million football fields. Brazil's Araticum Initiative has already restored 27,000 hectares of the Cerrado, the world's most biodiverse tropical savannah, working with over 180 partner organizations across nine states. Saudi Arabia's National Greening Program has restored 1 million hectares and planted 159 million trees so far. And in the Sahel, a WFP-led initiative across Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Niger has already restored more than 335,000 hectares, benefiting over 4 million people facing drought and displacement.
