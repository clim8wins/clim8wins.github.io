India's non-fossil power capacity has passed 300 GW, according to the country's renewable energy ministry: 300.5 GW as of July 31, over 60% of its 500 GW target for 2030 and more than half of all installed capacity. A record 55.29 GW was added in 2025–26, including 44.6 GW of solar. Prime Minister Narendra Modi pointed to the progress on November 2 as he opened the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit in New Delhi, where officials say about US$220 billion of investment is needed by 2030. The figures are the government's own and count large hydro and nuclear as non-fossil, and the investment is an estimate of need, not committed money. Still, over half of India's installed power capacity is now non-fossil, a step for the climate.
