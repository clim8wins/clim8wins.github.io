The Public Utility Commission of Texas ruled on October 1 that Southwestern Public Service, an Xcel Energy subsidiary, can close its 1.1 GW Tolk coal plant near Lubbock and build 1,100 MW of wind, 189 MW of solar and 322 MW of batteries in its place, according to the Sierra Club. New Mexico regulators set the closure for March 31, 2029, three years ahead of the 2032 date agreed in 2020 but a few months after the utility's December 2028 proposal. The caveats: only the Sierra Club reports the Texas ruling and the order itself wasn't available to check, and the plan also adds gas. Texas approved two gas peaker plants under cost caps, and New Mexico approved 2,088 MW of gas alongside the renewables in a $9.38 billion, 3.8 GW expansion that SPS says meets demand growth, with one commissioner dissenting. Still, coal out and wind, solar and batteries in is a step for the climate, though a partial one while new gas plants are built alongside.
