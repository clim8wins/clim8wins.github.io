State Grid Corporation, which operates the power network across most of China, has committed to connecting an average of 200 gigawatts of new wind and solar capacity every year through 2030 — roughly a quarter of the world's current annual wind and solar additions, from one grid operator. The plan is backed by 4 trillion yuan ($553 billion) in investment, aiming to lift renewables past 30% of generation while meeting all new electricity demand with clean power. To manage the buildout, State Grid is building 15 new ultra-high-voltage transmission lines, nearly doubling cross-provincial capacity to 500 gigawatts, and adding 260 gigawatts of storage — while retrofitting coal plants to run as low as 35% of rated output, freeing up grid space for renewables.
