The Global Carbon Project projects that global fossil CO₂ emissions will fall 0.4% in 2026, to 38.2 billion tonnes, in its annual carbon budget released on November 10 at the COP31 climate summit. Oil emissions are projected to fall 1.9%, which the authors say is likely a short-term response to the energy shock from the Iran conflict, while coal is projected to rise 0.6%. Solar and wind avoided about 4.1 billion tonnes of CO₂ in 2025, up from 0.28 billion in 2010, and 59 countries emitted less in 2025 than in 2019 while their economies grew. A one-year dip is not a peak: total CO₂ including land use is projected to rise to a record 43.3 billion tonnes, the figures are projections from a pre-print awaiting peer review, and scientists say the fall is partly driven by geopolitical shocks. Still, solar, wind and EVs making a visible difference to global emissions is a step for the climate.
