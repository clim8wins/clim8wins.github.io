Global petrol car sales now represent less than half of total new car sales. Cars running on petrol alone, with hybrids counted separately, made up 49% of new cars sold worldwide in the first half of 2026, down from 73% in 2021, according to Mobility Global data first reported by Nikkei. Petrol-only sales fell 10%, while battery-electric sales rose 12% and hybrids 10%. The caveats: it's six months of data taken during a fuel price shock and could reverse, hybrids and battery-electric cars together still sold less than petrol-only cars, most new cars still have an engine, and the whole market shrank. Still, fewer petrol-only cars is a step for the climate, though a long way from fewer engines.
