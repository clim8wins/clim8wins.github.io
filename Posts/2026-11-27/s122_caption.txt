England plans a new national forest, a proposed 700 square mile stretch from Milton Keynes into Cambridgeshire, under a Trees Action Plan published on November 26. The plan also commits to 3,700 hectares of new public woodland by March 2030, with over £1 billion for trees and woodland this parliament, as England aims to raise tree cover from 14.9% to at least 16.5% by 2050. A £53 million Community Trees Fund, announced earlier, opens early next year. The caveats: the forest's boundary and name aren't agreed and the government gives differing sizes, a national forest is a landscape rather than solid woodland, it isn't stated how much of the money is new, and planting reached only 74% of target from 2020–21 to 2024–25. Still, more trees near where people live is a step for the climate, once they're planted and looked after.
