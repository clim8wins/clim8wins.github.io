The European Investment Bank Group says its new Adaptation Plan Phase 2 aims to provide at least €30 billion for climate adaptation by 2030, which it says doubles its financing over the previous five years. The plan, endorsed by the bank's boards in October and due to be presented at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, combines finance, guarantees and advisory support for water systems, resilient farming, energy networks, public buildings and cities, with extra help for climate-vulnerable countries. This is a target from the bank's own releases: it's a floor, the earlier amount isn't given, it isn't clear whether the money is new, and bank president Nadia Calviño's claim that each euro spent saves 10 is unsourced. Still, funding to prepare for heat, floods and drought is a step for the climate, if it's delivered.
