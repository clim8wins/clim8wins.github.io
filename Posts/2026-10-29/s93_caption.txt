A new 132 MW solar park in Michigan is now operational, and customers who chose to pay for clean energy funded all of it. DTE Energy says Fish Creek Solar Park near Sheridan cost $268 million, paid for by customers in its voluntary MIGreenPower programme, and can power about 30,000 homes. In MIGreenPower, homes and businesses choose how much of their electricity to match with DTE wind and solar and pay a subscription fee on that share; nearly 100,000 homes and 1,900 businesses were enrolled in 2024, and their demand is funding six solar parks totalling 800 MW. Caveats: DTE's claim that other customers' bills are not raised and the 30,000-home figure are DTE's own, no emissions savings have been published, and the park opened later than regulators expected. Still, more clean power on the grid, paid for by customers who asked for it, is a step for the climate.
