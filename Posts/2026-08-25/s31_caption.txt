In 2009, a drill crew in Iceland accidentally punched into 900°C magma while searching for hot water — and instead of plugging the hole, engineers tested it. The well produced the hottest steam ever measured from a production well, with flow tests showing it could generate up to 36 megawatts, five to ten times a typical geothermal well. Now an international team is deliberately drilling new boreholes at the same site to study the magma-heat system on purpose, aiming to unlock supercritical water that can hold up to 10 times more energy than ordinary steam — a breakthrough that could multiply geothermal power output at volcanic sites worldwide.
