On August 26, South Korea's National Assembly passed two landmark climate laws: a new Climate Crisis Adaptation Act requiring climate risk forecasting and a "climate risk map" to guide government policy, and an amendment to the Carbon Neutrality Act setting legally binding emissions targets every five years through 2045 — 53-61% below 2018 levels by 2035, 69-80% by 2040, and 84-90% by 2045, en route to net zero by 2050. The amendment directly answers a 2024 Constitutional Court ruling that found the previous law unconstitutional for leaving no targets past 2030, which the court said "shifted an unfair share of the burden onto future generations." The reforms come under new President Lee Jae-Myung, whose government has also joined the Powering Past Coal Alliance and announced a coal phase-out date since taking office.
