A landmark 40-year satellite study just found something remarkable: the world's mangrove forests have stopped shrinking and are now growing back. After decades of heavy loss, regrowth started outpacing new losses around 2007 — leaving global mangrove cover just 1% smaller than in the 1980s, far better than previously feared. Denser, healthier "closed-canopy" mangrove forest has expanded too, now covering 58% of all mangroves worldwide, up from 50% in the 1980s. These coastal forests lock away carbon and shield shorelines from storms — so their comeback is a genuine climate win.
