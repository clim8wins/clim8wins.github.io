A new UN report shows how solar power can restore, not just occupy, degraded land — from old mining sites in South Africa to desert vegetation projects in China and dual-use solar irrigation for farmers in India. Renewable energy's global land footprint is set to grow sixfold by 2032, from 500,000 to 3 million hectares — but experts stress that where it's built determines whether it heals the land or harms it. Presented at the UN's COP17 land conference in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, it's described as one of history's largest land-use transformations, if done in the right places.
