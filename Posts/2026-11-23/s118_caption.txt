A UK start-up, Real Ice, is thickening Arctic sea ice by pumping seawater onto it in winter at Cambridge Bay (Ikaluktutiak), Nunavut, in Canada. A peer-reviewed paper reported in May 2026 on its 2024/25 winter trial found flooded areas were up to 32 cm thicker than untreated ice by the end of winter, and treated areas stayed brighter and appeared to melt more slowly through the melt season. The caveats: the paper comes from the Centre for Climate Repair working with Real Ice, 32 cm is a maximum, the current test site is 1 km² (about a quarter treated), why treated ice melts more slowly isn't understood, any cooling effect hasn't been measured, and ARIA warns brine could speed summer melt. Real Ice says it can only buy time while emissions fall. Still, thicker Arctic sea ice that stays brighter is a step for the climate, though a small one so far.
