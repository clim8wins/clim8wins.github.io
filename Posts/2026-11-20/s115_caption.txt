UNEP said at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya on November 12 that, since June, its satellite alert system (MARS) has confirmed fixes at five more major methane sources in Canada, Iraq, Kazakhstan and Mexico, which together account for an estimated 125,000 tonnes of methane. That brings the total to 46 major sources fixed in 13 countries. Tonne for tonne, the IPCC puts methane's warming effect at about 80 times that of CO₂ over 20 years, and about 27 to 30 times over 100 years. The figures are UNEP's, as reported by news outlets: as of June only 13% of its more than 5,000 alerts got a response, and just 9 of the 159 Global Methane Pledge countries have endorsed a new declaration to cut methane. Still, satellites helping stop real leaks is a step for the climate, though most alerts still go unanswered.
