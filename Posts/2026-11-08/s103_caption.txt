China's energy and industry CO₂ emissions fell 3.1% in the third quarter of 2026 compared with a year earlier, the biggest quarterly drop since the pandemic, according to analysis by Lauri Myllyvirta of the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, published by Carbon Brief. Solar, wind and nuclear output rose by 110 TWh, more than twice the growth in electricity demand, coal-fired power fell 3.3% and transport oil use fell 9%. Emissions have been flat or falling since March 2024. Clean power is not the whole story: mild weather, a weaker construction sector and the Hormuz oil shock also pulled emissions down, the analysis gives no error margins, and China is still building coal plants at near-record rates. Still, clean power outgrowing demand in the world's biggest emitter is a step for the climate.
