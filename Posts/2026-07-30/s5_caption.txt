China just released its climate targets for 2026–2030: a 17% cut in carbon intensity and a rise in non-fossil energy to 25% by 2030, up from 21.7% today. But it's not a clean win — independent analysts note China also changed how it calculates emissions, folding in industrial process emissions from cement and chemicals, which could let total emissions rise 3-6% even as the "intensity" target is met. No firm date was set for coal or oil to peak, either. As the world's largest emitter, China's trajectory still shapes the global path — we just think the full picture matters more than the headline.
