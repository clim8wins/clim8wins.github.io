Euronews reported on November 10 that Nukus 2, a 200 MW wind farm with a 100 MWh battery in Karakalpakstan, Uzbekistan, is nearing completion. Built by Saudi developer ACWA Power for about US$260 million, 25 of its 26 turbines are already generating, and the Asian Development Bank describes the battery as a first for Central Asia. ADB estimates the plant will produce about 728,000 MWh a year and avoid about 406,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year, but those are pre-construction estimates, the battery is still being commissioned until the end of November, and environmental assessments flagged possible impacts on land use and wildlife. Still, wind paired with batteries arriving in a new region is a step for the climate.
