Deforestation in Brazil's Amazon has fallen to its lowest level since records began in 1988. Brazil's space agency INPE estimates 3,335 km² of forest was cleared between August 2025 and July 2026, down 41.8% on the year before and 71% below the 2022 rate. Caveats: this is an annual estimate of clear-cutting only, so fire, drought and degradation are not counted, and El Niño-driven fires or bills in Congress that would weaken enforcement could still erode the gains. Still, one of the world's biggest carbon stores is being cleared far more slowly, a step for the climate.
