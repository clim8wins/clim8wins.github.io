As heatwaves pushed temperatures above 40°C across parts of Europe this summer, daily electricity demand spiked — up to 28% in Italy, 23% in Hungary, 14% in France, and 13% in Spain. Heat strained supply too: low Danube water levels forced nuclear cuts in Hungary, Alpine hydropower neared record lows, and Poland cut coal output on low Vistula levels. Solar was the only major power source that performed better than usual, generating 17% more than normal in France and Hungary, 5% more in Spain — and across June and July, solar supplied a record 25% of EU electricity, the first months on record. As Ember's Chris Rosslowe put it: "Solar is already doing heavy lifting during heatwaves, but the real challenge starts after sundown" — pointing to battery storage as the next piece of the puzzle.
