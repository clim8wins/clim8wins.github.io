At COP31 in Antalya on November 14, an international group led by Wetlands International, the UN Environment Programme and the Food and Agriculture Organization launched the Peatland Breakthrough, with 2030 targets to halt the loss of intact peatlands, restore at least 30 million hectares of degraded peatland and mobilise at least US$100 billion. Germany, Peru, Uganda and Ireland have joined, and Canada has endorsed it. UNEP's 2022 assessment says peatlands hold up to a third of the world's soil carbon, and the 12% that is drained causes about 4% of human-caused emissions. These are targets and a pledge, not delivered restoration: no funders for the US$100 billion are named and the countries' commitments aren't detailed. Still, protecting the planet's most carbon-rich soils is a step for the climate, if the money and the restoration follow.
