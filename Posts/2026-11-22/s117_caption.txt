Norway and Indonesia signed an agreement on November 11 at the COP31 summit in Antalya that lets Norway buy up to 12 million tonnes of verified emission reductions from new renewable energy projects in Indonesia over ten years, under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement. The Global Green Growth Institute signed for Norway and Indonesia's state utility PLN for Indonesia, with floating solar and batteries as the first focus, and Norway pays only after the cuts are achieved, independently verified and delivered. Coal supplies over 70% of Indonesia's electricity, according to the Norwegian government. Twelve million tonnes is a ceiling, no project is named yet, the final value is unconfirmed, and Norway can count the credits toward its own climate commitments under rules meant to prevent double counting. Still, carbon-market money for solar on a coal-heavy grid is a step for the climate, once projects are built and verified.
