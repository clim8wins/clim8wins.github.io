Malaysia's national utility Tenaga Nasional and plantation group IOI have signed a joint venture agreement to develop a 500 MW solar plant with a 250 MW / 1,000 MWh battery in Bahau, Negeri Sembilan. The venture has up to RM606.2 million of equity, with TNB holding 70%, and the project is to be supported by TNB's agreement with data centre operator DayOne, which is expected to buy up to 500 MW of renewable power over 21 years. Solar supplied only 2.4% of Peninsular Malaysia's electricity in 2025, against 58.6% for coal, according to a Reuters analysis of grid data. This is a signed agreement, not a built plant: no construction or commissioning date has been announced, no emissions estimate has been published, and the figures come from the companies' own filing and statements. Still, solar and storage built for data centres on a coal-heavy grid is a step for the climate, once it's built.
